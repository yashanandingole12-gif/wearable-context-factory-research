Wearable Devices as a Context-Sensing Layer for Human-Machine Interaction in Smart Manufacturing

Yash Anand Ingole

Department of Industrial Internet of Things, Priyadarshini College of Engineering, Nagpur, India

The Problem

Modern manufacturing integrates workers, cobots, and sensors, but there's an asymmetry gap:

  • Machines track production state, but lack direct human context
  • Static safety systems (light curtains) cause inefficient halts
  • Worker fatigue and strain go undetected
  • Cobots operate at uniform speed regardless of worker state

Proposed Solution

Hierarchical wearable-sensing architecture:

Layer 1: Multimodal wearables (IMU, ECG/PPG, temp, UWB)
Layer 2: Edge gateway for data cleaning & aggregation
Layer 3: Context inference engine with confidence scoring
Layer 4: Advisory HMI/HRI for adaptive cobot behavior

Research Questions

  1. RQ1: Can multimodal wearables provide real-time worker-state indicators?
  2. RQ2: Can confidence metrics prevent erratic robot behavior during packet loss?
  3. RQ3: Does sensor fusion detect hazards sooner than passive systems?
  4. RQ4: Can cobots adapt speed and ergonomics dynamically?
  5. RQ5: How do battery, dropout rates, and privacy affect adoption?
